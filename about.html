EIT Studio exists so that a well-validated proficiency measure is not abandoned for want of time or technical support.

What EIT Studio does

EIT Studio was built to make an elicited imitation task easier to implement. Transcription is automated, and the scoring procedure is made explicit: normalization rules are visible, editable, and exportable as JSON, so the exact configuration used in a study can be deposited with its materials and reused by others.

Research on automated scoring of elicited imitation is only now getting under way. McGuire and Larson-Hall (2025) demonstrate Whisper transcription with word error rate for English, Isbell et al. (2023) commercial speech recognition with edit-distance metrics for Korean, and Kim et al. (2024) form-based scoring with commercial natural language processing services. Together these studies suggest that the approach is promising.

However, each also asks for a degree of technical skill: API accounts, a scripting environment, and enough Python or natural language processing background to adapt code to one's own data. EIT Studio lowers that barrier by delivering the same procedure as a web-based tool, so that a researcher can attend to the methodology rather than the implementation.

The wider aim is straightforward. Research conclusions are only as good as the measurement behind them, and making a precise instrument quick to score removes one reason to settle for a rougher one. The recognition settings are fixed rather than exposed as options, so that scores remain comparable across studies; they are documented in full on the citation page, together with a methods-section template.

Where EIT Studio is going

Now

EIT administration and scoring, English

Bring your own sentence set: administer it in the browser on the schedule of the original design, with each response written out as its own audio file, and score the responses by word error rate against your targets — in one place.

Next

EIT in other target languages

Extension beyond English, once an appropriate scoring metric has been established for the language in question. That work depends on collaboration: with researchers who hold validated materials, and with collaborators who know the language well enough to judge what a scoring metric should treat as an error.

Next

Self-administered sessions

A link the participant opens at home, with the task presented and recorded in their browser on the schedule it follows in the laboratory. The hard part is the return trip: the responses have to reach the researcher, and this tool has no server to receive them.
