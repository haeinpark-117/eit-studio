About

Precise measurement,
within practical reach.

EIT Studio exists so that a well-validated proficiency measure is not abandoned for want of time or technical support.

The problem

Elicited imitation is efficient, language-general, and unusually well validated, but its weaknesses are practical rather than psychometric: scoring oral responses by hand is slow, and procedures vary across studies in ways that make findings hard to compare. Both problems are addressable.

The evidence page sets out what the validation literature reports, with figures.

What this tool does about it

It removes the manual transcription step, and it makes the scoring procedure explicit. Normalization rules are visible, editable, and exportable as JSON, so the exact configuration used in a study can be deposited with its materials and reused by others.

Automated scoring itself is not new — it has been demonstrated for English with Whisper and word error rate (McGuire & Larson-Hall, 2025), for Korean with commercial speech recognition and edit-distance metrics (Isbell et al., 2023), and for form-based scoring with commercial NLP services (Kim et al., 2024). What those demonstrations require is API accounts, scripting, and enough programming to adapt someone else's code; Kim et al. (2024) is a tutorial in Java. This tool provides the same procedure as a web page, so that the methodology rather than the implementation is what a researcher has to think about.

The wider aim is straightforward: research conclusions are only as good as the measurement behind them. Making a precise instrument quick to score removes one reason to settle for a rougher one.

The recognition settings are fixed rather than exposed as options, so that scores remain comparable across studies. They are documented in full on the citation page, together with a methods-section template.

What comes next

Now

Scoring, English

Automated transcription and WER scoring for any English elicited imitation task.

Next

Administration

Deliver your own task — your audio stimuli, your timing — and collect responses in the same environment.

Then

More languages

Added once an appropriate scoring metric has been established for each, rather than assumed to transfer from English.

On that last point: word error rate is computed over whitespace-delimited words and does not carry the same meaning in languages without word spacing, or in agglutinative languages where one particle error marks a whole eojeol wrong. Isbell et al. (2023) accordingly scored Korean with morpheme- and letter-based Levenshtein distances, reaching correlations of .94 to .96 with human scores. The approach generalizes; the metric has to be established language by language.

Contributing materials

Extending the tool to another language needs validated materials, not just a model that can transcribe it. If you have published an elicited imitation task in another language and would consider sharing it, please write.

  • Target sentence sets, with item numbers and syllable counts
  • Audio stimuli and the timing parameters used
  • Scoring rubrics or normalization conventions from the published study
  • Response data with human scores — for examining automatic–human agreement
  • Independent proficiency scores for the same participants — for examining concurrent validity against an external criterion

Contributors retain authorship and credit, materials are attributed to their original publication wherever they appear, and nothing is incorporated without explicit agreement. Inquiries about collaboration or validation studies are equally welcome.
