Elicited imitation tasks differ across studies in length, syllable range, and target structures. EIT Studio scores whichever instrument you actually use.

Bring your own sentence set

Paste or upload your targets as TSV, CSV, or one sentence per line. Any number of items. The 30-item English EIT of Ortega et al. (2002) is included as a preset.

›Reading the scoring table — column by column

One row per recording. Rows can be sorted by participant and item, by lowest accuracy, or by lowest confidence; the last two are the fastest way into the items that need checking.

ParticipantRead from the file name when it follows participantID_itemNN; otherwise whatever you assigned by hand in the file table.
ItemThe item number, which is what links the recording to a target sentence.
TargetYour target sentence after normalization — lowercased, punctuation stripped, scoring rules applied. This is the string the comparison actually uses, not the string you pasted in.
WhisperThe transcription after the same normalization, with the alignment against the target marked. Hovering a marked word shows what it was aligned to.
Accuracy100 × (1 − WER), in percent.
WERWord error rate: (S + D + I) / N over a word-level minimum-edit alignment, where N is the number of words in the normalized target. Truncated at 1.0.
ConfidenceThe mean probability of the tokens the model selected, shown as a percentage with the raw mean log probability beneath it.
AudioPlays that recording, so a questionable item can be checked without leaving the table.
›How the alignment is marked

The three edit types that enter the word error rate are marked in the transcription itself, so a score can be traced to the words that produced it.

white Substitution. A word produced in place of a target word. Hover to see the target word it replaced.
of Insertion. A word in the transcription with no counterpart in the target.
▲ Omission. A target word with no counterpart in the transcription. Hover to see which word is missing.
unmarkedA match, which contributes nothing to the error rate.
Target (7 words)
the streets in this city are wide
Whisper
the streets in the city are white

Two substitutions, no insertions, no omissions, over seven target words: WER = 2 / 7 = .286, accuracy = 71.4 %.

Because normalization is applied to both strings before comparison, capitalization, punctuation, and whichever variants you configured never affect the score. A response that is correct but differently punctuated scores as a match; a response that substitutes a grammatical morpheme does not.

›What the numbers do and do not license

Accuracy has a floor

Word error rate is truncated at 1.0, following the standard definition. A response with more errors than there are target words therefore scores 0 % rather than a negative value, so very disfluent responses are not distinguished from silent ones by the score alone.

Confidence is not a probability of being correct

Confidence is internal to the model: the mean probability assigned to the tokens it chose. It is comparable across items processed in the same session with the same model and decoding settings, and not comparable across checkpoints or decoding strategies. A high figure means the model was not hesitating, which is not the same as being right.

The combination is what is informative. Low accuracy with high confidence usually means the participant really did produce something different from the target. Low accuracy with low confidence more often points to the recording — background noise, a clipped onset, a boundary in the wrong place. Those are the rows to open the audio on.

An empty transcription is ambiguous

An item with no recognized speech is scored WER 1.0 and 0 % accuracy. That is correct if the participant said nothing, and wrong if a segment boundary was misplaced. Inspect these directly rather than reading them as non-responses.

Automatic scores still need a human subsample

Whisper transcribes toward an intended reading and can normalize morphosyntactic detail toward more expected forms — precisely the deviations an elicited imitation task measures. The speech recognition page sets out the evidence, and the citation page gives a methods-section template that includes reporting the agreement with a human-verified subsample.

›Checking efficiently, and what the exports contain

The table is built for triage rather than for reading every row. Sort by lowest accuracy to see where the transcription and the target diverge most, and by lowest confidence to see where the model itself was least certain; the two orderings surface different problems. Play the audio for any row you are unsure about.

Export to JSON when you intend to correct by hand: each item carries a transcription_corrected field, a flag field, and a reviewed marker, so a corrected transcription is recorded alongside the automatic one rather than replacing it.

CSV

participant, item, target, whisper, wer, accuracy_pct, confidence, avg_logprob, duration_s, file, model, rules

JSON

The same item-level fields, plus the alignment operations under ops, and a meta block recording the Whisper model, the decoding description, how confidence was computed, the full scoring-rule configuration, the number of target items, and a timestamp.

Privacy by architecture

Participant audio never
leaves the computer.

Speech recognition runs inside the browser itself. There is no server to send recordings to, because there is no server.

No upload, no account

Nothing to sign up for and nothing transmitted. Open the page and work.

Runs on your GPU

Whisper large-v3-turbo via WebGPU where available, with a WASM fallback. Speed therefore depends on your machine: the model is downloaded once (about 1.5 GB) and cached, and a 30-item set takes roughly five to ten minutes on a recent laptop.

Easier ethics approval

Because identifiable speech data stay on the researcher's own machine, no third-party processor needs to be declared.

Methodological transparency

What the numbers mean.

Automatic scoring is only useful if you can state precisely what it did. Every quantity the tool reports is defined here and reproducible from the exported rule file.

Automatic transcription does not replace human verification

WER is computed over a word-level minimum-edit alignment as (S + D + I) / N, truncated at 1.0, following the standard definition. Confidence is the mean token log probability and is comparable within a session but not across different checkpoints or decoding strategies. Reporting a human-verified subsample alongside automatically scored data remains good practice.
