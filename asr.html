Speech recognition

Why Whisper,
and with which settings.

Speech recognition was, for a long time, the reason automated elicited imitation did not work. The choice of model, and the way it is configured, is therefore part of the measurement rather than an implementation detail.

Why Whisper large-v3-turbo

Why Whisper large-v3-turbo

Elicited imitation needs a recognizer accurate enough on accented, disfluent speech to be used without being told what was expected. Earlier systems reached usable accuracy on learner responses only through tuning that often included forced alignment, which constrains the recognizer to the words of the prompt — self-defeating here, since a recognizer that can only output the target sentence will report the target sentence, and the deviations that constitute the measurement disappear. Four properties Radford et al. (2023) report make Whisper usable without any of that.

AccuracyError rates on standard benchmarks that approach human transcription, and that are competitive with fully supervised systems
Scale and breadthTrained on 680,000 hours of multilingual, multitask supervision rather than on a single curated corpus of read speech
RobustnessTrained to generalize across recording conditions, accents, and speech styles rather than to one variety, which is what makes accented learner speech and recordings made on participants' own phones and laptops usable
No adaptation neededUsed zero-shot, without dataset-specific fine-tuning — and, as above, without forced alignment, which is what makes the scores meaningful

The turbo variant is large-v3 with its decoder layers reduced, keeping the same encoder and, per its model card, only minor quality degradation while running several times faster — which is what lets it run inside a browser rather than on rented hardware. Reaching Whisper through a commercial API is not expensive, at USD 0.006 per minute of audio for OpenAI's whisper-1 endpoint, but it requires an account, a token, and a script, and the recordings leave the researcher's machine. The turbo weights are openly available, so the same model runs from Python or here in the browser at no cost and with nothing uploaded. The smaller whisper-small model remains selectable for checking that a file set is configured correctly, but it should not be used for scoring.

How it performs on learner speech

What Whisper is asked to transcribe matters more than any other single factor: O'Connor Russell et al. (2024) found it at 3.12 % word error rate on read audiobook speech but 19.4 % on telephone conversation and 26.7 % on videoconferencing speech. Elicited imitation responses sit at the favorable end of that range — short single sentences, recorded under controlled conditions, with no overlapping talk. On read speech from learners specifically, McGuire (2026) compared five current systems on 2,400 recordings from the L2-ARCTIC corpus and found Whisper large-v3 lowest in match error rate (.054), though performance varied by first language, from .007 for US English controls to .124 for Vietnamese speakers. Accuracy is not the whole story, though: Whisper normalizes as it transcribes, regularizing morphosyntax and omitting disfluencies rather than reporting them verbatim (Shen et al., 2026; Zusag et al., 2024), which is why a human-verified subsample remains necessary where the deviations are the measurement.

What to check in your own data

Two things support treating word error rate as a proficiency measure, and both belong in a methods section. The first is consistency with human raters: transcribe and score a subsample by hand and report how closely the automatic scores track it. The second is the relationship with an independent proficiency measure — a standardized test, a C-test, or another established instrument. Agreement with human raters establishes only that the transcription is faithful; it is the external correlation that supports word error rate as a proxy for proficiency rather than as a transcription statistic. Agreement of the first kind has been reported for this method — McGuire and Larson-Hall (2025) found an ICC of .929 between human and automatic error rates across 900 English elicited imitation responses — but that compares two ways of scoring the same responses. The external correlation is what each study has to establish for its own sample.

If the model changes

Speech recognition models improve, and this tool will adopt better ones when the evidence supports it. Because that would alter the scores, the model name and version are printed beneath every result table and included in the exported files, and should be reported in any study. Should the default model change, the previous version will remain selectable so that an analysis can be reproduced exactly as it was originally run.

The recognition settings used in EIT Studio

These are fixed rather than exposed as options, because varying them would make scores incomparable across studies. Reporting them is what allows a reader to reproduce the procedure.

Model onnx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo, run in the browser through transformers.js. Encoder in fp16 and decoder quantised to q4 on WebGPU; WASM at q8 where WebGPU is unavailable.
Decoding Greedy search — do_sample = false, num_beams = 1, temperature = 0. The task calls for verbatim repetition of what the participant produced, so the decoding must be deterministic: the same recording yields the same transcription every time. Sampling would introduce run-to-run variation into the score itself, and beam search would let a globally more fluent hypothesis override a locally accurate one.
No prompt, no bias The recognizer is never given the target sentence, and no vocabulary or initial prompt is supplied. This is the single most important setting: constraining recognition toward the expected words would make the transcription converge on the target and erase the deviations that constitute the measurement.
No cross-item context Each recording is decoded independently, with no conditioning on previously decoded text. One item's transcription therefore cannot influence the next, and the order in which files are processed does not affect the result.
Language Fixed to English (language = "en", task = "transcribe"). Leaving language detection to the model risks it identifying a strongly accented response as the speaker's first language: Ballier et al. (2024) found intermediate French learners of English identified as speaking French in 80 % of cases. Fixing the language removes that failure mode. The translate task is never used.
Audio Resampled to 16 kHz mono, the rate Whisper operates at, with a cap of 128 generated tokens — ample for a single-sentence response and a guard against runaway repetition.
Confidence Not produced by the model directly. A logits processor records the distribution at each decoding step, and the reported figure is the mean log probability of the tokens actually chosen, shown both as a probability and in raw form. It is comparable within a session but not across checkpoints or decoding strategies.
