Speech recognition

Why Whisper,
and with which settings.

Speech recognition was, for a long time, the reason automated elicited imitation did not work. The choice of model, and the way it is configured, is therefore part of the measurement rather than an implementation detail.

Why Whisper large-v3-turbo

Recognizing second language speech

For most of the history of speech recognition, systems were built around clear first-language speech, and accuracy fell when the speaker was a learner. That is the practical reason automated elicited imitation did not work: the responses a task collects are exactly the kind of speech recognizers handled worst. Earlier attempts to automate the task relied on systems that required extensive tuning to reach even modest accuracy on learner responses.

Those adjustments often included forced alignment: constraining the recognizer to the words of the prompt. For elicited imitation this is self-defeating. A recognizer that can only output the target sentence will tend to report the target sentence, and the very deviations that constitute the measurement disappear. The task needs a recognizer that transcribes what was said without being told what was expected.

What Whisper changed

Radford et al. (2023) report four properties of Whisper that matter for this task.

AccuracyError rates on standard benchmarks that approach human transcription, and that are competitive with fully supervised systems
Scale and breadthTrained on 680,000 hours of multilingual, multitask supervision rather than on a single curated corpus of read speech
RobustnessTrained to generalize across recording conditions and speech styles, so recordings made on participants' own phones and laptops remain usable
No adaptation neededUsed zero-shot, without dataset-specific fine-tuning — and, as above, without forced alignment, which is what makes the scores meaningful

Why the turbo variant

Whisper large-v3-turbo is large-v3 with its decoder layers reduced, retaining the same encoder and, per its model card, only minor quality degradation while running several times faster. That difference is what allows the model to run inside a browser on an ordinary laptop rather than on a rented GPU. McGuire and Larson-Hall (2025) processed their 900 responses through a commercial API on a remote T4 GPU, at a cost of about USD 0.23 and requiring a Python script and an API token; the turbo variant removes both the cost and the token, at the price of running on the researcher's own hardware. The smaller whisper-small model remains selectable for quickly checking that a file set is configured correctly, but it should not be used for scoring.

How it performs on learner speech

Whisper is not uniformly accurate, and what it is asked to transcribe matters more than any other single factor. O'Connor Russell et al. (2024) benchmarked six systems across three corpora and found Whisper at 3.12 % word error rate on read audiobook speech but 19.4 % on telephone conversation and 26.7 % on videoconferencing speech, where commercial systems roughly halved that error. Swain et al. (2026) report the extreme case: on spontaneous dyadic conversation spanning 26 first- and second-language varieties of English, Whisper models without fine-tuning produced word error rates of 58–96 %, with gaps of 92–124 points between the best and worst accent groups.

Elicited imitation responses sit at the favorable end of that range. They are short, single sentences, produced in a controlled recording, with no overlapping talk — closer to read speech than to conversation. On read speech from learners specifically, McGuire (2026) compared five current systems on 2,400 recordings from the L2-ARCTIC corpus and found Whisper large-v3 lowest in match error rate (.054), though performance varied by first language, from .007 for US English controls to .124 for Vietnamese speakers. Error rates for all five systems were substantially below those reported for the systems used in earlier work.

Two findings worth knowing before you rely on it

First, Whisper's language identification is not reliable for learner speech, and can return the speaker's first language rather than the target language. Ballier et al. (2024) found that the L1 of intermediate French learners of English was identified in 80 % of cases, while advanced learners were identified as speaking English in 90 %. EIT Studio therefore fixes the language to English rather than leaving detection to the model. The same study found that the subtoken probabilities Whisper assigns carry information about learner level, which is part of why a confidence figure is reported for every item.

Second, Whisper does not always transcribe verbatim. Shen et al. (2026) show that its normalization extends beyond orthographic conventions into morphosyntax: transcribing Spanish determiner–noun sequences, Whisper large-v3 regularized grammatical gender violations toward the prescriptively correct form for regular nouns, while repeating the same violations verbatim for nouns whose gender marking varies in usage. Whisper also tends to omit fillers, repetitions, and other disfluencies (Zusag et al., 2024), and performs less well on non-native than native accents, with proficiency negatively predicting error (Graham & Roll, 2024). For elicited imitation, where deviations from the target constitute the measurement, this is the property to keep in view: an automatic transcription is not a neutral record of what was produced, and a human-verified subsample remains necessary.

What to check in your own data

Verify a subsample by hand and report the agreement. Items where the participant produced nothing are worth inspecting directly, as are items containing the structures a study is designed to measure — those are where normalization would do the most damage. None of this is a reason to avoid the method; it is the ordinary reporting practice that makes an automated procedure auditable.

If the model changes

Speech recognition models improve, and this tool will adopt better ones when the evidence supports it. Because that would alter the scores, the model name and version are printed beneath every result table and included in the exported files, and should be reported in any study. Should the default model change, the previous version will remain selectable so that an analysis can be reproduced exactly as it was originally run.

The recognition settings used in EIT Studio

These are fixed rather than exposed as options, because varying them would make scores incomparable across studies. Reporting them is what allows a reader to reproduce the procedure.

Model onnx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo, run in the browser through transformers.js. Encoder in fp16 and decoder quantised to q4 on WebGPU; WASM at q8 where WebGPU is unavailable.
Decoding Greedy search — do_sample = false, num_beams = 1, temperature = 0. The task calls for verbatim repetition of what the participant produced, so the decoding must be deterministic: the same recording yields the same transcription every time. Sampling would introduce run-to-run variation into the score itself, and beam search would let a globally more fluent hypothesis override a locally accurate one.
No prompt, no bias The recognizer is never given the target sentence, and no vocabulary or initial prompt is supplied. This is the single most important setting: constraining recognition toward the expected words would make the transcription converge on the target and erase the deviations that constitute the measurement.
No cross-item context Each recording is decoded independently, with no conditioning on previously decoded text. One item's transcription therefore cannot influence the next, and the order in which files are processed does not affect the result.
Language Fixed to English (language = "en", task = "transcribe"). Leaving language detection to the model risks it identifying a strongly accented response as the speaker's first language; fixing it removes that failure mode. The translate task is never used.
Audio Resampled to 16 kHz mono, the rate Whisper operates at, with a cap of 128 generated tokens — ample for a single-sentence response and a guard against runaway repetition.
Confidence Not produced by the model directly. A logits processor records the distribution at each decoding step, and the reported figure is the mean log probability of the tokens actually chosen, shown both as a probability and in raw form. It is comparable within a session but not across checkpoints or decoding strategies.
